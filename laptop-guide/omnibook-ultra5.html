Pros & Cons

Pros

  • +Touchscreen + 4-mode flip — the biggest differentiator; use as a laptop, tent for presentations, tablet for annotating documents, or reverse for media
  • +Standard x86 Windows — every MIS enterprise tool works: SAP, Oracle, SQL Server, Tableau, company VPNs — no asterisks or compatibility research needed
  • +4.7/5 stars from 939+ reviews — highly rated for overall performance, portability, and battery life at Best Buy
  • +Battery life — tested at nearly 18 hours in light-use scenarios; one of the longest in this category for an Intel machine
  • +Excellent port selection — 2 USB-A ports (rare on this class of laptop), Thunderbolt 4, USB-C, and HDMI; rarely need a dongle
  • +5MP webcam — noticeably better than most laptop webcams for video calls
  • +Arc 130V GPU — handles Game Pass and lighter Steam games; full Game Pass bundle value is redeemable
  • +USB-C PD charging — no proprietary barrel connector; shares charging with phone and Steam Deck

Cons at current $949.99 MSRP

  • ✗Poor value at $949.99 — at this price you're getting a weaker CPU (Ultra 5 vs Ultra 7), weaker GPU (Arc 130V vs 140V), half the RAM, and half the storage compared to the ASUS Vivobook — all at essentially the same effective price after the Microsoft bundle
  • ✗HP MSRP inflation — this laptop has sold for $499–$599 on sale; $949.99 is an inflated anchor price to make discounts look impressive
  • ✗Mushy trackpad — the most consistent complaint across all reviews; noticeably inferior to the ASUS's trackpad
  • ✗Keyboard flex — some flex under heavy typing; less satisfying than premium competitors
  • ✗IPS display (not OLED) — 400 nits and standard color gamut; noticeably less vivid than the ASUS Vivobook's OLED
  • ✗Only 16 GB RAM — adequate for typical use today but will feel limited for heavy MIS workloads in 3–4 years
  • ✗Charging port caveat — only the Thunderbolt 4 port charges the laptop; the second USB-C on the same side does not, and it's easy to accidentally plug into the wrong one

The box claims 65–100W PD support but firmware caps intake near 65W. For travel, a 65W GaN charger works perfectly and is much smaller than the included brick.

MIS Career Fit

Standard x86 Windows means this laptop is fully enterprise-compatible on day one at any job. SAP, Oracle, Tableau, SQL Server, Power BI Desktop, company VPNs, proprietary IT tools — all work without compatibility research. The touchscreen and flip modes add genuine value for annotating requirements documents, whiteboarding with teams, and presenting in tent mode.

  • +Every enterprise software tool works — no ARM compatibility concerns
  • +Thunderbolt 4 dock connects monitors, keyboard, and Ethernet through one cable
  • +Tablet mode and stylus for annotating requirements docs and design mockups
  • +Tent mode for presenting reports and dashboards to small groups
  • !16 GB RAM is adequate today but may feel limiting for heavy data workloads in 3–4 years — the ASUS Vivobook (Option A) or HP Ultra 7 (Option C) are better long-term choices for data-heavy roles

Verdict

✗Do not buy at $949.99 MSRP. At this price the Ultra 5 model is outclassed by both the ASUS Vivobook S14 (better specs, cheaper) and the HP OmniBook X Ultra 7 (stronger CPU and GPU for only ~$100–$200 more). HP's inflated MSRP is a pricing tactic — this laptop has sold for $499–$599 before and will again.

If this laptop comes back on sale at ≤$599, it becomes an excellent deal — particularly when combined with the $639.86 Microsoft bundle, making it effectively free. The touchscreen + flip versatility, full x86 Windows compatibility, outstanding battery life, and good port selection make it a strong student and early-career laptop at the right price.
