Cons

  • ✗Only 16 GB RAM — the main weakness versus the ASUS Vivobook S14; adequate for typical MIS use now but may feel limiting for heavy data workloads in 3–4 years
  • ✗Only 512 GB storage — will fill up faster than the ASUS's 1 TB; plan to upgrade the SSD in 1–2 years if you store large files
  • ✗IPS display (not OLED) — 400 nits and standard color gamut; good but not as vivid or deep-black as the ASUS Vivobook's OLED
  • ✗Mushy trackpad — the most consistent complaint in reviews; noticeably inferior to premium trackpads on competing laptops
  • ✗Keyboard flex — some flex under heavy sustained typing; less satisfying than the ASUS Vivobook
  • ✗Charging port caveat — only the Thunderbolt 4 port (rear-most, left side) charges the laptop; the second USB-C on the same side does not, making it easy to accidentally plug into the wrong port
  • ✗Runs hot under sustained load — Tom's Hardware noted heat under intense sustained workloads; fine for typical use
  • ✗HP MSRP inflation — verify you're buying at a realistic sale price, not the inflated MSRP; check HP.com and Walmart for the best deal

!Correct port matters: Plug into the Thunderbolt 4 port (rear-most connector on the left side) to charge. The second USB-C connector on the left side is data/display only. The included 65W adapter works best — third-party adapters above 65W may show "weak charger" warnings due to firmware limits.
🔌Steam Deck synergy: One 65W GaN USB-C charger (~$25) powers both this laptop and your Steam Deck LCD — one brick in your bag instead of two. Look for Anker, Baseus, or UGREEN 65W GaN chargers.
